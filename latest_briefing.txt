Bei hohen Wellen und starkem Wind hat ein Kreuzfahrtschiff in Bremerhaven zwei Containerschiffe gerammt. Insgesamt verlief die Sturmnacht im Norden relativ glimpflich. Doch für den Mittag gibt es weitere Sturmflutwarnungen.

Ein Kreuzfahrtschiff ist bei stürmischem Wetter und hohen Wellen in Bremerhaven mit zwei Containerschiffen zusammengestoßen. Beim Auslaufen wurde die "Mein Schiff 3" nach Polizeiangaben von Schleppern unterstützt, als eine Böe das Schiff erfasste. Es sei daraufhin auf zwei Containerschiffe zugetrieben, die an der Stromkaje festgemacht hatten. An dem kleineren Containerschiff sei ein leichter Schaden entstanden, bei dem größeren ein erheblicher. Auch das Tui-Schiff wurde erheblich beschädigt. Auf Bildern waren kaputte Rettungsboote zu sehen.

Zunächst war von einer Kollision zwischen dem Kreuzfahrtschiff und einem Containerschiff die Rede. Später teilte die Polizei mit, es seien insgesamt drei Schiffe betroffen. Tui Cruises bestätigte einen Unfall seines Schiffes. Zum Unfallzeitpunkt waren einer Sprecherin zufolge 2.428 Gäste und 922 Crewmitglieder an Bord.

Verletzte gab es nicht. Mindestens zwei Schiffe wurden beschädigt. Zu dem Unfall kam es beim Auslaufen in Bremerhaven.

[2] WAHLEN IN BRASILIEN: EINE NACHT ZWISCHEN JUBEL UND MISSTRAUEN

Feuerwerk und Freudentränen - in Brasilien feiern Anhänger den Wahlsieg von Lula als Erfolg der Demokratie. Doch während die einen aufatmen, warnen andere vor dem politischen Kampf, der jetzt erst beginnt.

Es war etwa 20:30 Uhr Ortszeit, als bei Lulas Anhängern der Jubel ausbrach. Das, was vor wenigen Wochen noch niemand für möglich gehalten hatte, passierte: Der linke Amtsinhaber ging bei der Auszählung in Führung und baute seinen Vorsprung immer weiter aus.

Freudentränen, Feuerwerk, feiern wie während des Karnevals - auch auf der Para São Salvador in Rio de Janeiro. "Brasilien, Souveränität, Demokratie, wir haben gewonnen, Brasilien hat gewonnen", jubelt eine Frau.

Ein Lula-Anhänger ist aber auch skeptisch. Es sei ein triumphaler Moment. Doch der Kampf ende hier nicht. "Warum? Weil Bolsonaros Leute die Mehrheit im Kongress und im Senat haben und bereits erklärt haben, dass sie das Ergebnis dieser Wahl nicht akzeptieren werden."

Der linksgerichtete Staatschef Lula da Silva hat die Präsidentschaftswahl in Brasilien gewonnen.

Lula ist eine historische Wende gelungen. Noch nie hat es ein Kandidat seit 1989 geschafft, einen Rückstand im ersten Wahlgang in einen Sieg zu verwandeln. Die Brasilianer hätten dafür gestimmt, "die Demokratie zu sichern", sagte Lula sichtlich gerührt und geschafft.

Er bedankte sich zunächst bei seinem Vizepräsidenten Gerardo Alckmin. Dieser gab den Dank direkt zurück: "Mein tiefer Respekt. Sie haben zum zweiten Mal die brasilianische Demokratie gerettet."

Wer beherrscht die Sozialen Netzwerke? Diese Frage könnte in Brasilien wahlentscheidend sein.

Etwa zehn Millionen neue Wählerstimmen konnte der fast 81-jährige Lula im Vergleich zum ersten Wahlgang hinzugewinnen. Das dürfte auch mit der massiven Mobilisierung seiner Anhänger in den vergangenen Tagen zu tun haben - sowohl auf der Straße, als auch per Social Media. Die Wahlbeteiligung war historisch hoch.

Lula sagte ergriffen: "Diese Wahlen haben mir viele Lektionen erteilt, wirklich viele Lektionen." Er versicherte, seine Regierung werde die Art und Weise, wie dieses Land regiert wird, ändern.

"Ich möchte Ihnen sagen, dass wir das Volk stärker an unseren Entscheidungen beteiligen werden und, dass die digitale Kommunikation in meiner Regierung einen sehr großen Stellenwert einnehmen wird", sagte Lula.

Bolsonaros Familienname bleibt sein wichtigstes politisches Kapital - und gleichzeitig seine größte Schwäche.

Wenige Minuten später, tritt Flávio Bolsonaro in Brasilia mit versteinertem Blick vor die Kameras. Das Ergebnis der Wahl werde er nicht anerkennen. Das sei unmöglich unter den gegebenen Umständen. Er werde die Annullierung dieser Wahlen beantragen, sagte Bolsonaro.

"Das ist hier alles ein ziemliches Spielchen. Ich werde nicht hinnehmen, dass die Spielregeln mitten im Spiel erneut von Lulas Freunden am Obersten Gerichtshof geändert wurden." Es sei unerklärlich, dass so etwas geschehen konnte.

Was ist passiert? Das Oberste Gericht hatte die Stimmabgabe am Sonntag landesweit um zwei Stunden verlängert. Hintergrund waren allerdings Beschwerden beider Kandidaten beim Wahlgericht, das die Entscheidung mittrug. So meldete Lulas Wahlkampfteam Probleme mit dem Nahverkehr im Bundesstaat Bahía. Dies habe dazu geführt, dass Wähler nicht zu den Wahllokalen gekommen seien.

Auch Bolsonaro hatte sich für eine Verlängerung der Stimmabgabe ausgesprochen. Er verwies auf starke Regenfälle im Süden und Südosten Brasiliens, wo er besonders großen Rückhalt genießt. Verlängert werden konnte die Wahl allerdings nur in den Lokalen, in denen die Urnen noch nicht geschlossen waren.

Der Präsident des Wahlgerichtes Kassio Nunes Marques erklärte, es gebe keinen Grund, den Wahlprozess infrage zu stellen.

Nach dem Sieg in der ersten Wahlrunde war das Bolsonaro-Lager siegessicher in die Stichwahl gezogen - das sei alles nicht normal, findet Bolsonaro-Wählerin Larissa Abreu aus Barra da Tijuca. Leider gebe es große Zweifel am gesamten Wahlprozess. "Erneut erleben wir so einen Tag, das schürt große Zweifel und Misstrauen", sagte sie.

Bolsonaro geht als Favorit in die Stichwahl um die Präsidentschaft Brasiliens. Seine Wähler hoffen auf einen wirtschaftlichen Neustart.

Lulas Sieg bremst eine Welle rechter Wahlerfolge in Lateinamerika und durchkreuzt Hoffnungen von US-Präsident Donald Trump auf einen weiteren Verbündeten in der Region.

Trotz des Wahlsiegs steht Präsident Lula nun vor massiven Herausforderungen, sagt der Politologe Oliver Stuenkel von der Carnegie Stiftung für Internationalen Frieden in Washington. Vor allem, weil Bolsonaros Liberale Partei stärkste Kraft im Kongress ist.

"Die linken Parteien haben im Kongress, insbesondere im Senat, an Gewicht verloren, es wird schwer, regierungsfähige Mehrheiten zu finden", sagt Stuenkel. Auch das regionale Umfeld sei äußerst ungünstig. Die große Mehrheit der Nachbarländer werde von rechten Präsidenten regiert.

"Zudem gibt es eine bilaterale Krise mit den Vereinigten Staaten, die den Druck etwas durch Sanktionen oder Zölle noch mal erhöhen könnten", erklärt der Politologe. "Es ist also eine sehr schwierige Situation, in der die Erwartungen - auch angesichts der großen Unsicherheit, die derzeit in Europa herrscht - tatsächlich sehr gering sind."

[3] MARKTBERICHT: ANLEGER VERSUCHEN DAX ZU STABILISIEREN

Die Zahl der Sorgen bleibt groß. Neben den anhaltend hohen Ölpreisen dämpfen die Sorgen um den KI-Boom die Stimmung. Jetzt hoffen die Anleger auf gute Unternehmensbilanzen und Hilfe von den Notenbanken.

Der Broker IG Markets taxiert den deutschen Leitindex vor dem Start des Xetra-Handels 0,2 Prozent höher auf 25.238 Punkte. Am vergangenen Freitag war der DAX mit einem Minus von 0,7 Prozent auf 25.181 Punkten aus dem Handel gegangen.

"Zu den Sorgen über die hohen Energiepreise gesellen sich die Sorgen über möglicherweise zu teure Bewertungen der KI-Unternehmen", sagt Portfoliomanager Thomas Altmann vom Vermögensverwalter QC Partners. Auf Wochensicht hatte der deutsche Leitindex 1,7 Prozent eingebüßt. Um zu einer Jahresendrally anzusetzen, müssten für die DAX-Bullen deutlich positivere Vorgaben kommen, damit sich die Stimmung aufhelle. Bis dahin bleibe Sand im Getriebe, unterstreicht Jens Klatt, Marktanalyst bei XTB.

Auch zum Start der neuen Handelswoche sind die Anleger spürbar verunsichert. Die Liste der Sorgen und Probleme ist lang: Der Ölpreis bleibt hoch, und die Frage, wie lange der KI-Boom noch anhält, ist immer noch ungeklärt. In der neuen Woche warten die Anleger auf die Bilanzen der Tech-Riesen Alphabet, Microsoft und Amazon.

Fallen die Zahlen stark aus, dürfte das zwar vorerst beruhigen. Aber die hohen Erwartungen können leicht enttäuscht werden, mit turbulenten Folgen für die Aktienmärkte.

Die bisher uneinheitliche Berichtssaison zeige, dass viele Unternehmen mit den schwierigen wirtschaftlichen Rahmenbedingungen zu kämpfen hätten, beurteilt Marktanalyst Andreas Lipkow vom Broker CMC Markets die Lage. Die Börsen bräuchten inzwischen mehr als nur gute Nachrichten, nämlich handfeste Belege für nachhaltiges Wachstum.

Aus dem DAX legen im Wochenverlauf unter anderem der Chemiekonzern BASF, die Deutsche Bank sowie die Autobauer Mercedes-Benz und Volkswagen Quartalszahlen vor.

Neben der Bilanzsaison wird die Geldpolitik ein wichtiges Thema sein. In den kommenden Tagen stehen die Zinsentscheidungen der US-Notenbank Federal Reserve (Fed) und der Europäischen Zentralbank (EZB) an. Auf die steigende Inflation hatten die Notenbanken mit Zinserhöhungen reagiert. Steigende Zinsen sind am Aktienmarkt unbeliebt. Sie sorgen dafür, dass viele Anleger vermehrt in Rentenpapiere investieren, um von den höheren Zinsen profitieren. Deshalb hoffen die Aktienmärkte, dass die Währungshüter die Zinsen unverändert lassen.

Die Fed tagt nur sechs Tage vor den US-Zwischenwahlen", meint Chefstratege Robert Greil von der Privatbank Merck Finck. "Die weiter steigende US-Inflation spricht eigentlich klar für eine Leitzinserhöhung, dennoch dürfte sich die Fed so kurz vor den Wahlen eher zurückhalten, wie sie das historisch meist gemacht hat." Auch von der EZB erwartet Greil am Donnerstag keine Zinsänderung. Bei beiden Notenbanken sei jedoch eine weitere Erhöhung im Dezember wahrscheinlich.

Die asiatischen Aktienmärkte haben zum Wochenauftakt im Vorfeld der Notenbanksitzungen und Firmenbilanzen überwiegend schwächer tendiert. In Tokio gab Nikkei-Index 0,4 Prozent auf 62.431 Punkte nach.

Auch in China hielten sich die Anleger zurück: Die Börse Shanghai verlor 0,2 Prozent auf 3.683 Stellen. Der Index der wichtigsten Unternehmen in Shanghai und Shenzhen fiel um 0,5 Prozent auf 4.083 Punkte.

[1] WALLHACK M-001 REVIEW: WHEN FUN OVERRIDES SPECS

Batteries. They're not typically thought of as a significant source of innovation. Most companies have spent the last few years removing mice in favor of more lightweight designs.

That's what's made the return of the replaceable and rechargeable battery surprising—something I once thought to be a lost technology from a bygone era. A few companies have recently realized that the ability to pop out and replace your battery in seconds is a great way to extend mouse life without needing to increase battery size or efficiency.

From the handful of mice I’ve tested with hot-swappable batteries so far, Wallhack does it best. The mouse is designed around the function’s ease-of-use, and everything else comes together to make a mouse that feels not only competitive for gaming, but also well thought-out and satisfying to use in everyday life.

The M-001’s shape is a standard right-handed, symmetrical design. This means the mouse’s left and right sides are shaped identically, but only the left side has thumb buttons. There are two main buttons, two side buttons, a standard ratcheting scroll wheel, and a power switch on the underside.

With a length of 120 mm, the M-001 is slightly shorter than contemporaries like the Razer Viper V4 Pro (125 mm) and Logitech Pro Superstrike X2 (127 mm). The overall width and height are comparable, at 63.5-mm tall and a 39-mm wide. The largest difference is the profile of the mouse: The front end sits significantly lower than other mice, and the rear has a more significant hump, which creates a more angled shape overall. When using a palm grip, this puts your hand at a slightly more aggressive forwards angle, with your palm sitting higher and your fingers pointing more downwards. It’s a slight difference, but I found this angle to be better for quick movements compared to a more “flat” design. However, like most shape-related choices, this is ultimately down to individual preference. (To compare mouse sizes, I typically use a website like eloshapes)

The two main buttons are lightly sculpted, with subtle concavity for fingers to comfortably rest in. Similarly, there is a subtle taper at the bottom center of the mouse which makes lifting and moving easier. The side buttons use a simple, rounded shape placed just above the taper, making it easy to rock your thumb upwards to press them without getting in the way. They’re just elevated enough that the buttons don’t recess into the shell when pressed. The only shape-related issue I had was the scroll wheel, which is a good bit more elevated than other mice. This isn’t a huge issue, and I adapted to it after a few hours of use, but it’s something I could see being off-putting for users accustomed to a lower-profile scroll wheel.

The most important part of this mouse’s construction is the magnesium shell, which provides significantly more rigidity than the plastic shells used on most mice. Because of this, the mouse feels incredibly sturdy and solid. The metal shell also gives the mouse a cool, slick feeling underneath your fingertips, which I prefer to the matte plastic feeling that most mice have.

Despite using metal, it only weighs 53.6 grams, making the M-001 quite light by most standards, It still falls slightly behind the Viper V4 Pro’s 50.3 grams and lags way behind Corsair’s superlight, 36-gram Sabre V2 Pro Ultralight. But the M-001 is incredibly solid, with none of the flexibility or flimsiness that most plastic mice have. When compared to other magnesium mice like the AngryMiao Infinity .97 or Corsair’s magnesium-shelled Sabre, the M-001 has an entirely solid upper shell without any cutouts or holes, which feels much better for regular use with a multitude of grip styles (plus, it’s much less likely to have dust, dead skin, or anything else build up inside of it). While the full magnesium shell increases weight, it’s a worthwhile trade-off for a smooth and rigid shape.

It’s also worth noting that some of Wallhack’s promotional material depicts the mouse as being a solid black color, but this isn’t quite the case. The unit I received is much closer to a gunmetal color, with a significant amount of metallic flake across the finish. The color looks fantastic in person, but it's difficult to properly capture in images.

Flipping over this mouse reveals one of the more atypical design decisions being made: the lack of a bottom shell. Instead of an entire underside, there is a thin frame around the circumference of the mouse that holds six feet in place. Between them is a chasm that reveals the entire PCB and battery.

Of particular note is the PCB’s function inside the mouse: Instead of simply sitting inside of the shell, the PCB works as a primary structural component. Similar techniques have been used in designing supercars and sport bikes, with the Ferrari F50 using the engine block as a stressed member that provides rigidity to the entire car, a technology borrowed from Ferrari’s F1 team. This provides some obvious benefits to rigidity and weight-savings, but it’s also just undeniably cool to be able to flip over your mouse and see the entire circuit board.

Right behind the PCB is the star of the show, a hot-swappable battery. While I’ve tested quite a few mice with this technology, none of them have managed to make the battery-swapping so satisfying. Instead of being attached with magnets or a rotating locking mechanism, the batteries use a custom-made -spring-loaded ejector similar to an SD card or a Nintendo Switch cartridge. To insert the battery, simply press until you feel a click, and it will lock into place. To remove it, do the same: press until you feel a click, and it will pop out slightly. Of all the mice I’ve tested with removable batteries, this is by far my favorite mechanism to use: It’s intuitive, fun, and feels sturdy.

This battery system does come with a trade-off. Because hot-swappable batteries means an effectively-infinite battery life (as long as you remember to plug the old one into the dongle when you swap), Wallhack has decided to entirely remove the USB-C port on the mouse. This means it only functions as a 2.4G wireless mouse and cannot be plugged or charged in directly. This isn’t a huge issue (it never inconvenienced me, since there was always a fully charged battery ready to swap in), and it theoretically means the connection quality will be better, since there’s fewer components between the antenna and the receiver. But it is worth noting that, if a battery dies or your dongle craps out, there’s no other way to use this mouse.

In every high-polling mouse I’ve reviewed, the dongle has been a bit of a pain point. A high polling rate necessitates proximity, which in turn requires in a small thing sitting on your desk. In most cases, these things have only one or two functions: They connect to the mouse, and they maybe have a small number of LED indicators (between 1 and 3) that can display battery life, connection quality, or some other setting. Almost every time, they’re small and light enough to slide around on your desk and will inevitably end up falling behind it once or twice due to the weight of the cable pulling it back.

With the M-001, nearly all of these issues are resolved: The dongle is given purpose, function, and I’d even argue dignity. Instead of the small, existing-by-necessity designs that most manufacturers ship with their mice, Wallhack has managed to make a beautiful piece of industrial design. The dongle is a medium-sized metal wedge sporting a gunmetal gray finish identical to the mouse. It has two camera-like dials up front, a button, and a slot for a rechargeable battery. The DPI dial features numbering from 400 to 25,600, and the polling dial goes from 125 Hz to 8,000 Hz.

The dials both have LED indicators next to them, which illuminate alongside the mouse to indicate a setting being changed. When rotated, they both have a satisfying ratcheting mechanism that’s stiff enough to avoid accidental inputs, without being difficult to turn using a single finger.

While I loved these dials from the moment I saw them, I really didn’t expect to use them much. I’ve always been more of a set-and-forget type of person. But after testing this mouse for a while, I regularly found myself using both of the dials: On-the-fly DPI adjustments were great for games like Marvel Rivals. I tended to prefer, for example, a higher DPI for Thing and a lower for Thor. Polling adjustments, too, were useful in situations where older games (like Destiny 2) got grumpy when I was using a mouse above 1,000 Hz.

Like many mice today, the M-001 uses an in-browser customization client. Wallhack’s web client, Wallhack Terminal, takes a unique stylistic approach, relying on a black-and-white, text-based system that resembles Command Prompt more than a typical gaming software. This is both a breath of fresh air stylistically, and much more straightforward than some of the software I’ve used in the past.

The configurator has an extensive feature set, including adjustments for mouse rotation, acceleration, and lift-off distance, which is adjustable by millimeters instead of the vague Low/Medium/High that some softwares use. The sensor can also be pushed to scan faster than the mouse’s 8,000 Hz polling rate, which can potentially improve the accuracy of the sensor, but limits battery life.

Along with adjusting DPI and polling with the dials on the dongle, switching them to the red “DPI” And “Hz” text allows for more granular adjustments within the configurator.

The sensor inside the mouse is a Pixart PAW3950. For anyone who doesn’t have an exhaustive list of mouse sensors floating around their head, this is the same type of sensor used in the 2022 Razer Viper V2 Pro (branded as the Razer Focus Pro 30k). That's four years old at this point. It’s not quite a cutting-edge sensor anymore. But unless you're in the top-tier of competitive play, the sensor will feel consistent and reliable enough, being able to easily track and manage direction changes during fast-paced shootouts. My personal daily driver is a Viper V2 Pro with the exact same sensor (I prefer the shape to the V3 and V4), and it’s still perfectly serviceable after four years of use.

The main buttons inside this mouse use Omron Optical switches, while the side buttons opt for low-profile Omron blue-dot mechanical switches. The scroll wheel’s encoder is a mechanical unit as well, which means it doesn’t have quite the same longevity as the optical units some mice are beginning to use. However, they are easier to replace or fix if something does go wrong.

The internal design of this mouse is impressively simple: There are six feet on the underside, and the outer four all have screws underneath. When you remove these four screws, the bottom half of the shell comes off cleanly (no flimsy plastic clips!), and a few more screws separate the internals from the top shell.

I’ve said many times that, when it comes to peripherals, technical specs are only one part of the story. More than that, it’s important to have a device you enjoy using. Of all the mice I’ve tested, the M-001 is by far one of the most enjoyable. The shape is comfortable and natural for multiple grip styles, and the material ensures that the mouse feels rigid and stable despite its light weight. The dongle is a brilliant design that elevates itself from a sad desk tchotchke to a practical and functional device. And the battery mechanism is one of the most clever and fun designs I’ve seen in ages.

Everything about this mouse is deeply satisfying, and I found myself excited to use it every time I sat down. Of course, this mouse isn’t at the cutting edge of sensors or switches, and because of this, I can’t exactly recommend this mouse to someone looking for an ultra-cost-effective option with min-maxed specs. But in using the mouse on the daily, I didn't find that it mattered as much as you might think. 8,000 Hz polling and a 25,600 DPI sensor are enough for all but the pros. Instead, it uses tried-and-true internals that function perfectly well and, more importantly, have crisp and precise tactile responses.

[2] SCIENTISTS TRAPPED AN ANTIMATTER PARTICLE FOR MONTHS TO STUDY ITS SECRETS

Antimatter is not known for being easy to get ahold of. That’s part of why these particles remain so cryptic. But recently, a team of physicists at Northwestern University managed to trap a single antimatter particle for months. Cooling it to temperatures near absolute zero, they could control its quantum states with great precision without even touching it. The experiment, published in Physical Review Letters, represents a breakthrough in our understanding of one of the great mysteries of physics.

In theory, antimatter should be everywhere. Mathematics predicts that every particle of matter has an equivalent antiparticle, a fact that scientists have verified experimentally. The universe should therefore contain similar amounts of both, but almost everything we observe is made up of ordinary matter, and we still don’t know why.

It is possible to produce antimatter particles in laboratories, but studying them is still incredibly difficult. When an antiparticle comes into contact with ordinary matter, the two annihilate each other and release energy. To prevent research facilities from exploding, scientists use vacuum chambers and electromagnetic fields that suspend the antiparticles in isolation. But this only partially solves the problem. Studying these particles, especially experimenting with their behavior on a quantum scale, remains challenging.

For this experiment, researchers at Northwestern trapped a positron, the antimatter counterpart of electrons. The team, led by Gerald Gabrielse, developed a device known as a single-positron quantum cyclotron, which used electric and magnetic fields to hold the positron in a vacuum at a temperature close to absolute zero. This means the antimatter particle was at its lowest energy state, allowing the researchers to induce controlled changes in its quantum behavior. Unlike other methods in particle physics, this approach didn’t destroy the particle. The researchers were therefore able to keep the single antiparticle trapped for months, allowing them to repeat measurements.

So what can taming antimatter particles actually teach us? Scientists know that matter and antimatter have very similar properties. For example, an electron and a positron have exactly the same mass, although their electric charges are opposite. However, questions still remain about exactly how similar these two forms of matter are and whether there are small differences that have gone unnoticed in experiments conducted to date.

To this end, researchers want to compare the magnetic properties of both particles. Physicists have already made very precise measurements with electrons, but achieving the same level of precision with positrons has proven more difficult. According to the American Physical Society, the new device could enable measurements of antimatter particles about 30 times more precise than scientists were capable of before.

So far, the experiment has not revealed any unexpected differences between the two particles. What the researchers have achieved is an improvement in their ability to detect these differences in antimatter particles. If future measurements find that a positron responds to magnetic fields differently than an electron, scientists would have to revise some of the laws they use to explain the behavior of matter and antimatter.

Folding phones are more fragile than normal phones. Folding phones have more parts than a traditional smartphone, not to mention a hinge. Companies advertise how many folds these devices have been tested for—usually around 200,000 or 500,000 folds—but a single drop can be incredibly damaging. They are not as dust-resistant, and some aren't as water-resistant (only the Pixel 11 Pro Fold and iPhone Duo are IP68-rated in the US), so you'll want to be especially careful. Use a case, and it may be worth investing in the manufacturer's device care policy.

Don't remove the built-in screen protector. Most of these folding phones have a film over the inner screen. You might be tempted to remove it, but don't—it can damage the display. The packaging usually includes instructions not to remove it and how to care for your device.

There's a small learning curve to the software. Flip-style folding phones are fairly straightforward, but book-like folding phones that double as tablets have new multitasking tricks that will take some getting used to if you want to make the most of them. I recommend taking some time to run through these features so you understand how they work.

They're expensive. Do you need one? Nobody needs a folding phone. If you think you'll benefit from one, know they're frequently discounted during big sale events like Black Friday, and you can also knock a few hundred dollars off if you trade in an eligible device.

Folding flip phones are for people who want a compact device. Think of a normal phone you can fold in half. That's pretty much what you get, plus a screen on the front to monitor widgets and texts. This does mean you can flip the phone to answer a call and flip it shut to end one—super cool. You can put the phone at a 90-degree angle, and some apps will adapt to show controls on the bottom and important details at the top, like in video calling apps or when watching videos on YouTube, though this depends on the phone. Folding flip phones typically have mediocre battery life, and their cameras aren't as good as their non-folding counterparts.

Folding book-like phones are for people who want the big-screen experience. Are you constantly watching full-length movies on your phone? Or do you always find yourself jumping back and forth between two apps? Then these are for you. They have good battery life and better cameras (though not as good as non-folding phones), but they're far more expensive. These include the short-lived triple-screen folding devices like Samsung's Galaxy Z TriFold. They're thick and heavy, though we're seeing newer models that are surprisingly thin. Still, you may have a hard time fitting them in your pockets.

If you're team Android, this is a good time to buy. Samsung and Google just updated their foldables, and Motorola released its first big foldable in the summer and won't announce the Razr 2027 lineup until the spring. For folks outside the US, we probably won't see new foldables from Honor or Oppo until the spring, too.

If you're considering an iPhone, Apple is likely to unveil a new folding phone soon, so you might want to wait and see what it looks like.

Whichever style you go for, my favorite feature of folding phones is the ability to see the viewfinder on the external screen and snap a selfie with the high-quality rear cameras—just set the phone down slightly folded so it stays upright, and there's usually a button you can click in the camera app to send the preview to the external screen. You can usually trigger the shutter hands-free with a palm gesture. (Motorola's Razr Ultra lets you start and stop video recordings with hand gestures, too!) It's excellent when traveling if you want to take a photo of yourself next to a point of interest.

Many of these phones offer similar functionality with translation—Samsung and Google, for example, let you show your words translated on the external screen so the person you are speaking to can see it, and then they can tap a button on the screen to have the phone start translating their words, which you'll see on the internal screen. It's a much nicer system than using Google Translate on a candy bar phone. —Julian Chokkattu
